Reading it: The blue delta beside a date is the shift from the current baseline as you move levers. Cushion is business days between the IFR (or the IFC, once it is in hand) and the need date — ahead, ≤3 days, behind. Per-sheet dates: Every sheet carries its own IFR, IFC and IFF. Edits cascade downstream — move a sheet's IFR and its IFC and IFF follow; edit IFC and only IFF follows; edit IFF and it holds. Anything you haven't touched keeps tracking the levers. reset returns all three to plan. A set is gated by its latest sheet at each milestone. Construction need: Set it once on a sheet set and every sheet inherits it, or give a sheet its own date (shown amber) when it differs. Cushion is business days from that sheet's IFR (IFC once in hand) to its need date. In hand: Tick in hand beside an IFR, IFC or IFF date only when we actually hold that drawing. A ticked date is recorded as an actual and pins its date — levers and scenarios no longer move it, so committed deliveries stay put while the rest of the plan flexes. The in-hand count per set is the same signal the Revit MCP feed writes back as % complete. Export CSV writes one row per work package — WPID, sheet no., plan vs delivery date, status, slip, fab release, cushion, and the scenario settings used — ready to paste back into the FORMA_READY workbook. Assumptions: weekends excluded, holidays not modeled; IFC→IFF lag is a placeholder until real fab + ship durations are set.
