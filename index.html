Reading it: The blue delta beside a date is the shift from the current baseline as you move levers. Cushion is business days between IFC and the need date you enter — ahead, ≤3 days, behind. Per-sheet dates: Every sheet carries its own IFR, IFC and IFF. Edits cascade downstream — move a sheet's IFR and its IFC and IFF follow; edit IFC and only IFF follows; edit IFF and it holds. Anything you haven't touched keeps tracking the levers. reset returns all three to plan. A set is gated by its latest sheet at each milestone. Construction need: Set it once on a sheet set and every sheet inherits it, or give a sheet its own date (shown amber) when it differs. Cushion is business days from that sheet's IFC to its need date. Forecast vs in hand: Marking a package In hand records it as an actual and pins its date — levers and scenarios no longer move it, so committed deliveries stay put while the rest of the plan flexes. The in-hand count per set is the same signal the Revit MCP feed writes back as % complete. Export CSV writes one row per work package — WPID, sheet no., plan vs delivery date, status, slip, fab release, cushion, and the scenario settings used — ready to paste back into the FORMA_READY workbook. Assumptions: weekends excluded unless toggled, holidays not modeled; IFC→IFF lag is a placeholder until real fab + ship durations are set.
